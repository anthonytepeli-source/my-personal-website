About Me

My Research Journey

My interest in physics and astrophysics began in childhood with a simple curiosity about how the Universe works. At university, that curiosity led me into computational physics, where my first research experience grew from statistical thermodynamics and molecular dynamics at the Computational Unit of UDSM-DUCE. I developed skills in C++, Python, Bash, and MATLAB for modelling, analysis, and visualization, and my research later expanded into molecular simulation, quantum physics, and computational chemistry, with work recognized at the 11th UDSM Research and Innovation Week and by COSTECH.

Alongside computation, I have pursued observational astronomy, including the IRAM 30-meter School on Millimetre Astronomy, where I trained in radio astronomy, observing, data processing, and interferometry, with practical exposure to GILDAS/CLASS and NOEMA. These experiences have given me both computational and observational foundations, and I now hope to bring them together in studying the violent and transient Universe.
